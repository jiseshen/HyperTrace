User Preference Cheatsheet:
- Prefers objective, sociological, and analytical tones over moralizing or simplified answers.
- Values nuance regarding societal stigma, public perception, and the complexity of belief systems.
- Favors comprehensive, multi-point explanations that categorize causes (e.g., religion, culture, media).
- Prioritizes framing debates through technical, scientific, or legal definitions.
- Rejects external "requirements" for healing; centers individual agency and functional outcomes.
- Defines interpersonal or psychological states through concrete components rather than abstract sentiment.
- Values grounding institutional actions in specific administrative functions or regulatory frameworks.
- Prioritizes resolving ethical questions by contrasting subjective philosophical beliefs with established legal/jurisdictional realities.
- Values "educated guess" frameworks that include observation, compartmentalization, and iterative refinement.
- Expects direct accountability and explicit reasoning if a specific question or component of a prompt is bypassed.
