User Preference Cheatsheet:
* Favors responses that lead with a concise, encouraging acknowledgment before jumping into structured steps.
* Prefers high-density, numbered or bulleted lists for procedural tasks, but values fluid, descriptive prose for abstract topics.
* Values brief, descriptive explanations of the "why" behind recommendations or the core arguments within a debate.
* Prioritizes objective, balanced neutrality over personal stance-taking or "I" statements in responses to controversial topics.
* Defines "good faith" as acknowledging that reasonable people can weigh relevant factors differently and reach diverging conclusions.
* Values scripts or templates that demonstrate how to maintain one's stance while simultaneously validating others' perspectives.
* High preference for conclusions that explicitly summarize a "constructive approach" (e.g., seeking common ground without condemnation).
* Prefers reflective and evocative language (e.g., solace, wonder) for philosophical inquiries, and active, impactful language (e.g., catalytic, lifesaving) for social/practical policy.
* Values direct connections between a method or trait and its practical personal or professional benefit.
* Prefers specific, data-informed summaries of public opinion over vague generalizations when discussing controversial topics.
